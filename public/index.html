      A FEW THINGS WORTH KNOWING

      You don’t need to be a tech expert.

      Scam or spam — what’s the difference?

      Spam is an unwanted message, often an advertisement. A scam tries to trick you into giving money, private information or access. An unwanted offer is not automatically a scam.

      Someone wants my OTP. What should I do?

      Never share an OTP, PIN or password with someone who contacts you. If a request seems to come from your bank, open its official app or call the number on your card yourself.

      Does “no obvious warning signs” mean safe?

      No. A short, convincing or unfamiliar scam can pass these checks. The app cannot authenticate senders or know the whole conversation. Verify requests for money or personal details independently.

      Can you automatically protect my phone?

      This version checks messages you choose to share. Automatic SMS filtering needs a separate native mobile app and platform permissions. Gmail checking needs the optional desktop extension.

      ALREADY INTERACTED WITH A MESSAGE?

      Take the next step calmly.

      Stop replying. Save evidence. Contact the service through a trusted channel.

      I sent money or shared payment details

      Contact your bank or payment provider immediately using its official app or known number. In India, report financial cyber fraud at 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in. Keep transaction IDs and screenshots.

      Open India’s cybercrime reporting portal ↗
      I shared a password or secret code

      Use the official app or website to change the affected password, review active sessions and contact the provider. If it involves banking, contact the bank immediately. Never share another code with the caller.

      I opened a link

      Close the page. Do not enter information or install anything. If you downloaded an app or gave it access, remove the access and get help from the device maker’s official support. Opening a link alone does not always mean your device is compromised.
